# Unit-price evidence checks

Checked 2026-11-03. Source: [Australian Competition and Consumer Commission, Unit Pricing Code](https://www.accc.gov.au/business/industry-codes/unit-pricing-code). See also [the grocery retailer guide](https://www.accc.gov.au/about-us/publications/a-guide-to-unit-pricing-for-grocery-retailers).

UNIT-01 flags an active ranged product without a unit-price display evidence reference and checked date, only where the operator has explicitly marked the store as subject to unit pricing. The reviewer records the actual shelf, online or advertising evidence, or a documented product exemption. The software checks that the record exists, not whether the display meets the law.

The ACCC explains that coverage depends on the grocery categories sold and the kind and size of retailer. Display rules cover readability, prominence, accuracy, position and measurement units. Those facts require a real review. Do not turn on the flag for every store by default. Do not describe a zero finding count as compliance certification.

LOCAL-01 flags evidence older than 90 days. This is our default review interval, not a statutory deadline. The 14-day report-freshness rule and 60-day stock-cover threshold are also operating policy, not law. No New Zealand legal rule is inferred from this Australian source. Agree jurisdiction-specific requirements before using this for another jurisdiction.

No shelf-ticket publisher or regulatory filing ships. /compliance prepares a review list. /record-label records what an authorised person actually checked.
